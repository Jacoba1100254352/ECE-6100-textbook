\section*{The Thread Through the Book}\label{sec:auto-001}
A fast computer is a machine that spends less time waiting.
It keeps useful data nearby, overlaps independent work, and sometimes starts work before it knows whether that work will be needed.
Each improvement creates another question: what happens when the guess is wrong, the data is missing, or two processors want to change the same value?
Those questions connect the chapters that follow.

This is a course companion, not a replacement for the lectures or the current assignment specification.
Read the prose for the mechanism and work through the examples for its consequences.
The chapter questions and separate answer sheet test that reasoning; the formula reference, glossary, and final-review checklist help you revisit it.

\section*{Choosing a Reading Path}\label{sec:cross-chapter-reading-paths}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Build the foundation.} Chapters 1 and 2 introduce parallelism and performance. Chapters 3 and 4 show how caches, pipelines, and prediction turn those ideas into a working processor.
    \item \textit{Put the pieces together.} Use Chapter 5 alongside Chapters 3, 4, and 6 when interpreting simulator counters or combining cache and pipeline calculations.
    \item \textit{Find more overlap.} Chapter 6 lets hardware schedule independent instructions; Chapter 7 gives that job to the compiler. Read them together to compare what each scheduler must know.
    \item \textit{Move beyond one core.} Chapters 8 through 10 connect shared data, synchronization, and communication. Coherence answers which copy to read; consistency and synchronization constrain how different accesses fit together.
    \item \textit{Review selectively.} Use Appendix~\ref{ch:final-review-checklist} to locate weak concepts, then return to their explanations. Appendix~\ref{ch:advanced-topics} extends the architectural questions to quantum and superconducting machines.
\end{enumerate}
